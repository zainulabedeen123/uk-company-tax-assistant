# Paying yourself: salary, dividends, loans, pensions, expenses

Figures: `rates-2026-27.md` (tax year 2026/27). Use `salary-dividend-planner` for numbers.

## 1. Salary — common choices for a director-shareholder in 2026/27

| Salary | When it fits | Employer NIC | Employee NIC | Income tax (if no other income) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| £5,000 (secondary threshold) | Single-director company (no Employment Allowance) wanting zero NIC | £0 | £0 | £0 — but **no** state pension qualifying year (below LEL £6,708) |
| £6,708 (LEL) | Single-director company wanting a qualifying year at minimal cost | £256.20 (15% × £1,708) | £0 | £0 |
| £12,570 (Personal Allowance / PT) | Company **can** claim Employment Allowance (e.g. 2+ directors on payroll, or other employees) — employer NIC covered by EA | £1,136 before EA (15% × £7,570) | £0 | £0 |
| £12,570 without EA | Sometimes still marginally efficient because salary + employer NIC saves CT at 19%–26.5% | £1,135.50 | £0 | £0 |

Key logic: salary and employer NIC are deductible for Corporation Tax; dividends are not. Whether £12,570 beats £6,708/£5,000 without EA depends on the company's marginal CT rate — compute it rather than assuming. Salary must be paid (or credited to the director's loan account) and run through RTI. Directors without employment contracts are not entitled to NMW.

## 2. Dividends — legality and paperwork

- Only from **distributable profits** (accumulated realised profits less accumulated realised losses), CA 2006 s830, justified by "relevant accounts" (last annual accounts, or interim management accounts) — s836–s838. Consider unpaid Corporation Tax on current-year profits when assessing reserves.
- **Interim dividends**: declared by the directors under the articles (Model Articles art. 30); can be varied before payment; taxed when paid. **Final dividends**: recommended by directors, declared by ordinary resolution of members; a debt once declared; taxed when due.
- Paperwork: board minutes (and members' resolution for finals) recording the profits available and the amount per share; a **dividend voucher** for each payment (company name, date, shareholder, share class, number of shares, amount). Keep with company records.
- Dividends must be paid **pro rata** to shareholdings in the same class unless the articles allow otherwise (alphabet shares / dividend waivers — settlements legislation risk, ITTOIA 2005 s624; escalate).
- **Unlawful dividends** (paid without sufficient reserves): repayable by a shareholder who knew or had reasonable grounds to believe it was unlawful (s847); HMRC may treat as a loan (s455) or earnings. Directors may be in breach of duty.
- Dividends are not subject to NIC and do not need payroll. They are personal income: report via Self Assessment if total dividends exceed £10,000 or the director already files SA (company directors usually file).

### Dividend tax 2026/27
Dividends sit on top of other income. £500 Dividend Allowance (taxed at 0% but uses up the band). Then 10.75% (basic band), 35.75% (higher), 39.35% (additional). Scottish taxpayers use UK dividend bands.

Example: salary £12,570 + dividends £37,700 → £500 at 0%, £37,200 at 10.75% = £3,999.

## 3. Director's loan account (DLA)

- Record every transaction between the director and the company that is not salary, dividend or reimbursed business expense.
- Overdrawn DLA (director owes the company) at the period end and not repaid within **9 months and 1 day**: company pays **s455 tax** (CTA 2010 s455) at **35.75%** for loans made on/after 6 April 2026 (33.75% before). Report on **CT600A**. Refundable via s458 when the loan is repaid, released or written off — claim within 4 years of the end of the period in which repayment happened; refund paid 9 months and 1 day after the end of the period of repayment.
- **Bed-and-breakfasting anti-avoidance (s464C)**: repayments of £5,000+ reborrowed within 30 days are matched; where the balance is £15,000+ and there were arrangements to redraw, repayments are disregarded.
- **Write-off/release**: taxed on the individual as a dividend (ITTOIA s415), company still has Class 1 NIC exposure (treated as earnings for NIC); no CT deduction.
- Loans > £10,000 at any time in the tax year with interest below the official rate → benefit in kind (P11D), Class 1A for the company.
- Repaying a DLA by declaring a dividend or bonus is fine if properly documented and lawful.
- Credit DLA (company owes the director): director can withdraw tax-free; interest paid to the director is deductible for the company and taxable on the director (company deducts 20% income tax and files CT61 quarterly).

## 4. Employer pension contributions

- Paid by the company into a registered pension: deductible for CT if wholly and exclusively for the trade (normally accepted for director-shareholders when total remuneration package is reasonable); no NIC; not taxable on the director within the Annual Allowance (£60,000; tapered for adjusted income > £260,000; carry forward 3 years of unused allowance).
- Deduction only in the period **paid**.

## 5. Expenses the company can pay

- Wholly, exclusively and necessarily for the job (ITEPA s336) for the director; wholly and exclusively for the trade for the company.
- Use-of-home: £6/week flat (for employees working from home under homeworking arrangements) or a reasoned apportionment; rent-to-company arrangements need care.
- Business mileage in own car at 55p (first 10,000 miles) / 25p.
- Travel to temporary workplaces, subsistence on qualifying journeys, professional subscriptions on HMRC's list 3, training related to current duties.
- Not allowed: ordinary commuting, personal clothing, client entertaining (no CT deduction even if paid by company).

## 6. Self Assessment for directors

- Register by 5 October after the tax year if new to SA. Paper return 31 October; online return and balancing payment **31 January**; payments on account 31 January and 31 July (if last year's SA liability > £1,000 and < 80% collected at source).
- Penalties: £100 immediately late; £10/day after 3 months (max 90 days); 5%/£300 at 6 and 12 months; late-payment penalties 5% at 30 days, 6 months, 12 months (rates may change with SA penalty reform — **[verify]**).

## 7. Extracting value on exit

- Sale of shares: CGT; Business Asset Disposal Relief 18% from 6 April 2026 (lifetime £1m) if a 5% shareholder, officer/employee, trading company, conditions met for 2 years.
- Closing a solvent company: Members' Voluntary Liquidation → capital distributions (BADR possible) — subject to the Targeted Anti-Avoidance Rule (ITTOIA s396B) if the individual continues a similar trade within 2 years. Informal strike-off distributions capped at £25,000 for capital treatment.
- Escalate all exits.

## 8. Quick decision checklist for "how should I pay myself?"

1. Is the company profitable after CT? What are distributable reserves?
2. How many directors/employees on payroll (Employment Allowance eligibility)?
3. Director's other income (employment elsewhere, rental, pension) — determines bands.
4. Scottish taxpayer? (affects salary only.)
5. Does the director need a state pension qualifying year (salary ≥ £6,708)?
6. Child Benefit in household (HICBC from £60,000 adjusted net income) or income near £100,000 (Personal Allowance taper — 60% effective band)?
7. Pension contributions as an alternative to extra dividends.
Then run `salary-dividend-planner`.
