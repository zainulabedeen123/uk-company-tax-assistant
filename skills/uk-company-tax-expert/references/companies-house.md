# Companies House — company law obligations (England & Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland)

Legal basis: Companies Act 2006 (CA 2006); Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 (ECCTA). Figures: see `rates-2026-27.md`.

## 1. Key concepts

- **Company number** — 8 characters (SC prefix Scotland, NI prefix Northern Ireland).
- **Authentication code** — 6-character code posted to the registered office; needed for WebFiling. Being replaced over time by GOV.UK One Login account access; the "Companies House authentication code" remains in use for many filings.
- **Accounting reference date (ARD)** — the date the financial year ends. On incorporation it is automatically the last day of the month in which the anniversary of incorporation falls. Change with form AA01 (s392). A period can be shortened as often as needed (ECCTA reforms will restrict this from April 2028) and lengthened up to 18 months, but lengthening is limited to once every 5 years unless an exception applies (e.g. aligning with a parent/subsidiary, or the company is in administration).
- **Confirmation date / review period** — confirmation statement period is a maximum of 12 months, starting on incorporation or the last confirmation date.
- **Registered office** — must be an "appropriate address" (since 4 March 2024): a document sent there should be expected to come to the attention of a company representative, and delivery can be acknowledged. PO boxes are not allowed.
- **Registered email address** — mandatory for all companies since 4 March 2024. Not public. Provide on incorporation or at the next confirmation statement; change via the "update registered email" service.
- **Lawful purpose statement** — required on incorporation and in every confirmation statement: the company's intended future activities are lawful.

## 2. Annual accounts

### What to prepare (CA 2006 Part 15)
All companies prepare statutory accounts for each financial year, whether trading or not. They must give a true and fair view, be approved by the board, and the balance sheet signed by a director (s414). The directors' report must be approved and signed (s419) unless the micro-entity exemption applies.

### Filing choices by size (until the April 2028 reforms)
- **Micro-entity** (FRS 105): minimal balance sheet + notes; may omit P&L and directors' report from the filed copy. Prepared accounts are presumed true and fair.
- **Small company** (FRS 102 Section 1A): may file "filleted" accounts (balance sheet and notes only — no P&L or directors' report) or abridged accounts (requires all-members' consent). Audit exemption available if small (s477) unless excluded (e.g. public, banking/insurance) or members with ≥10% request an audit (s476).
- **Dormant company** (no significant accounting transactions — s1169): dormant accounts (form AA02 style), audit-exempt under s480. Paying Companies House fees is not a significant transaction.
- **Medium/large**: full accounts, strategic report, audit (unless exempt).

The balance sheet of exempt small/micro companies must contain the statutory statements: audit exemption under s477, members have not required an audit under s476, directors acknowledge responsibilities for records and accounts, and (if applicable) "prepared in accordance with the provisions applicable to companies subject to the small companies regime" / micro-entity provisions.

### Deadlines (s442)
- Private company: **9 months** after the end of the accounting reference period.
- Public company: 6 months.
- **First accounts** where the first period is more than 12 months: deadline is 21 months after incorporation (private) — i.e. 9 months from the first anniversary of incorporation — or 3 months after the period end if later.
- If the ARD is shortened (AA01), the new deadline is the later of the normal deadline and 3 months from the date the AA01 notice is given.
- Corresponding-date rule (s443): a deadline lands on the corresponding date in the target month; if the period ends on the last day of a month, the deadline is the last day of the target month. Example: year end 30 June → due 31 March.
- No grace for weekends/bank holidays: the deadline is the deadline.

### Extensions
Apply via the "Apply for more time to file your company's accounts" service **before** the deadline, giving a reason outside the company's control (e.g. illness, fire, cyber attack). Not granted for routine reasons.

### How to file today
- Micro, small (filleted), dormant: Companies House WebFiling / "File your accounts" online service, or commercial software (iXBRL).
- HMRC's joint "Company Accounts and Tax Online" (CATO) service closed on **31 March 2026** — accounts can no longer be filed together with the CT600 via the free HMRC service.
- Paper filing still possible for some accounts types until reforms.

### ECCTA accounts reforms — from April 2028 (announced 9 June 2026)
- All accounts must be filed via **commercial software in iXBRL**; web and paper accounts filing closes.
- Small companies and micro-entities must file a **profit and loss account**, with an option to opt out of *publishing* it.
- **Abridged accounts abolished**.
- Strengthened audit-exemption eligibility statement.
- All component parts of accounts to be filed together.
- Limits on how often the ARD can be shortened.
Directors' reports for small companies: the earlier proposal to remove them did not stop small companies preparing them; check current guidance before advising.

### Penalties
Automatic civil penalties (see rates file). Plus criminal offence for directors (s451). Persistent failure can lead to strike-off. Appeals only for exceptional circumstances outside the company's control; appeals rejected for e.g. first accounts, accountant's fault, not knowing the deadline, cannot afford to pay. Process: appeal → senior casework manager → independent adjudicator → Registrar.

## 3. Confirmation statement (CS01) — s853A

- File at least once every 12 months, **within 14 days after the end of the review period**. Can file early (resets the review period start).
- Confirms or updates: registered office, SIC code(s), statement of capital, shareholder information (for non-traded companies: names and shareholdings; ECCTA will require full names of shareholders — check current status), PSC information, officers, registered email address, and the lawful purpose statement.
- For confirmation statements filed on or after 18 Nov 2025, each director's Companies House personal code (proof of identity verification) is provided as part of the statement.
- Fee: £50 online, £110 paper — payable once per 12-month payment period (extra statements in the same period are free).
- Changes to officers, PSCs, registered office etc. **must not** wait for the confirmation statement — file the event forms within their own deadlines (below). Share allotments (SH01) and capital changes have their own forms; the confirmation statement can only update shareholder *details* for share transfers.
- Failure: criminal offence for company and every officer; can lead to strike-off. No automatic civil penalty but Companies House may begin strike-off action.

## 4. Identity verification (ECCTA) — mandatory from 18 November 2025

- Who: all **directors** (and equivalents), **people with significant control (PSCs)**, and anyone filing on behalf of a company (presenter verification postponed — will be introduced later). Corporate directors, LP general partners, officers of relevant legal entity PSCs: later phases.
- How: (a) directly with Companies House via GOV.UK One Login (photo ID check; app or online); or (b) through an Authorised Corporate Service Provider (ACSP) — an accountant/formation agent registered with Companies House and supervised for AML. Result: a **Companies House personal code** (11 characters), used to link the verified person to each company role.
- New directors appointed on/after 18 Nov 2025: must be verified **before** the appointment is filed (code included in AP01 / incorporation).
- Existing directors: provide the personal code with the company's **first confirmation statement filed on or after 18 Nov 2025**. The 12-month transition ends **18 November 2026**; after that every director must be verified.
- Existing PSCs: 
  - If also a director of the same company — provide the PSC code separately via "Provide identity verification details for a PSC" within the 14-day period starting the day after the company's confirmation statement date.
  - If not a director — within the first 14 days of their birth month.
  - New PSCs after 18 Nov 2025 — when added or within 14 days.
- Consequences: acting as a director without verifying is an offence (company and director); Companies House can reject filings; the register will show unverified status; civil financial penalties may apply.
- Deceased PSCs do not need verification; remain on the register until probate/letters of administration.

## 5. Event-driven filings and deadlines

| Event | Form | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Appoint director | AP01 (individual) / AP02 (corporate) | 14 days |
| Director leaves | TM01 | 14 days |
| Change director details (name, service address, residential address) | CH01 | 14 days |
| Appoint/terminate/change secretary | AP03 / TM02 / CH03 | 14 days |
| Change registered office | AD01 | Effective on registration; old address valid for service for 14 days after |
| Change ARD | AA01 | Before the filing deadline of the period being changed |
| Allotment of shares | SH01 | 1 month after allotment |
| New PSC (individual) | PSC01 | Update company PSC register within 14 days of confirming details; file at Companies House within 14 days after that |
| Cease PSC | PSC07 | as above |
| Change PSC details | PSC04 | as above |
| Statement of no PSC / updates | PSC08 / PSC09 | as above |
| Special resolution (e.g. change articles, change name) | RES / NM01 + resolution | 15 days |
| New articles | copy with resolution | 15 days |
| Register a charge (mortgage/debenture) | MR01 | 21 days from creation (beginning with the day after) — late = unenforceable against liquidator/creditors without court order |
| Satisfy a charge | MR04 | no statutory deadline |
| Purchase of own shares | SH03 (+ stamp duty) | 28 days |
| Cancel shares on buyback | SH06 | 28 days |
| Strike-off (voluntary) | DS01 | When eligible: not traded, changed name or disposed of property in last 3 months; notify all interested parties within 7 days |
| Withdraw strike-off | DS02 | as soon as ineligible |
| Restoration | RT01 (administrative) or court | within 6 years of dissolution |

## 6. Statutory registers the company must keep (or elect to keep on the public register)

Register of members, directors, directors' residential addresses, secretaries, PSCs, charges (optional now), plus minutes of board and general meetings (keep 10 years). ECCTA will abolish the requirement to keep registers of directors, directors' residential addresses, secretaries and PSCs (the central register will be the record) — check commencement status before advising.

## 7. Dormant companies

- Dormant for Companies House: no significant accounting transactions. File dormant accounts and confirmation statements every year.
- Dormant for HMRC: tell HMRC it is dormant (no CT600 needed unless HMRC sends a notice to file). If HMRC issues a notice, a return must be filed.

## 8. Striking off and dissolution

- Voluntary (DS01): company must not have traded or disposed of assets in the previous 3 months. Distribute or deal with remaining assets first — on dissolution all remaining property passes to the Crown (bona vacantia). Distributions in anticipation of strike-off: capital treatment for individuals only if total ≤ £25,000 (CTA 2010 s1030A); above that, treated as income (dividend) — consider an MVL.
- Compulsory: Companies House strikes off for failure to file accounts/confirmation statement; objections can be lodged.

## 9. Directors' core duties (CA 2006 ss171–177)

Act within powers; promote the success of the company; exercise independent judgement; reasonable care, skill and diligence; avoid conflicts of interest; not accept benefits from third parties; declare interest in proposed transactions. Plus: keep adequate accounting records (s386 — 3 years private / 6 years public; HMRC needs 6 years); file on time; when insolvency threatens, consider creditors' interests (wrongful/fraudulent trading, Insolvency Act 1986 ss213–214).

## 10. Other ECCTA powers to know

- Registrar can query, reject and remove inaccurate information; can annotate the register.
- Stronger name rules (no names facilitating dishonesty or implying false connection).
- Failure-to-comply civil penalties regime (up to £10,000 per contravention).
